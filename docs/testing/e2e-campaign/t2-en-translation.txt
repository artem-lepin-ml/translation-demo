In 221 BC, the Qin dynasty unified China. Qin Shi Huang abolished the feudal enfeoffment system, introduced the commandery-county system, and standardized the script, currency, and units of measurement. The policy of "one script for all writing, one gauge for all carts" laid the foundation for two millennia of centralized rule.

To repel the incursions of the northern nomads, Qin Shi Huang ordered the old fortifications to be connected and repaired, creating the Great Wall. Hundreds of thousands of laborers built the wall across mountain ranges, and countless workers died at the construction sites. The Great Wall later became one of the most famous symbols of China.

The Qin dynasty's tyranny provoked large-scale uprisings. In 206 BC, Liu Bang entered Xianyang, and the Qin dynasty fell. After four years of the Chu-Han contention, Liu Bang defeated Xiang Yu and founded the Han dynasty, making Chang'an his capital.

The Han dynasty inherited the institutions of the Qin but lightened taxes and punishments. Under Emperor Wu of Han, the state reached its zenith: Zhang Qian's missions to the Western Regions opened the Silk Road, and Confucianism became the official doctrine.

During the reign of Emperor Wu, Sima Qian composed the Records of the Grand Historian, chronicling three thousand years of history from the Yellow Emperor to Emperor Wu. The work established the model of biographical-annalistic historiography, and later generations honored Sima Qian as the "Sage of History".
