"In Lagash, one of the most economically prosperous city-states, these conditions culminated in a political overthrow. The local ruler concentrated near-absolute ownership of half of Lagash’s land in his own hands, systematically redistributed administrative offices to his personal retinue, and increased exactions on both dependent laborers and free commoners. Concurrently, elite-driven debt bondage among the common populace reached its peak. The ruling house was overthrown in a mass uprising, though the ruler himself was spared his life and civic rights. The popular assembly subsequently elevated a certain Uruinimgina to power, bestowing upon him the title of lugal (late 24th century BCE). This new ruler abolished the excessive levies, once again separated the temple economy from the ruler’s private estates, and annulled a series of debt contracts. Comparable reforms were occasionally implemented in other Mesopotamian city-states."
